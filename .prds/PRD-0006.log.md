## T-004 -- 2026-10-22

The flood tests (uat-005) started from the brief's bounds: an 8 MiB TCP round trip under the flood
within `max(4 × unloaded, unloaded + 5 s)`, and at least 90% of 200 exchanges (1 s each) answered.
Each test runs one session through `FakeRemote` and the real relay. The blaster sends 1 200-byte
datagrams in a tight loop and reads its echoes. The exchanging sender shares the blaster's UDP
forward and echo server, so its losses include drops in those sockets' kernel receive queues, which
nothing in sshunnel can share out fairly. All runs were debug builds, as `cargo make test` builds
them; `taskset` stood in for CI's 4-vCPU runners and a smaller one.

| Conditions | Runs | TCP unloaded | TCP under the flood | Worst ratio | Worst excess | Exchanges answered |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 128 CPUs | 10 | 31.9 to 70.5 ms | 35.2 to 97.1 ms | 1.50× | +31 ms | 198 to 200 |
| `taskset` 4 CPUs | 10 | 31.4 to 41.5 ms | 66.9 to 80.8 ms | 2.41× | +46 ms | 199 to 200 |
| `taskset` 2 CPUs | 5 | 47.8 to 49.3 ms | 103.6 to 132.9 ms | 2.78× | +85 ms | 200 |
| `taskset` 4 CPUs, `cargo llvm-cov` (CI's coverage job) | 5 | 29.4 to 64.1 ms | 68.8 to 271.9 ms | 4.24× | +208 ms | 199 to 200 |

The flood was saturated throughout: on 4 CPUs its flow carried about 18 000 echoes a second (12 666
to 13 803 during 676 to 776 ms of exchanges) while the blaster offered several times that. During
each TCP transfer it echoed 400 to 670 datagrams, about one per stream frame: the writer taking
turns.

Tuned once:

- **TCP:** the floor tightened from +5 s to +2 s, about ten times the worst excess (+208 ms, under
  coverage). The 4× factor stays: that same run reached 4.24×, so the floor is what carries an
  instrumented run.
- **Exchanges:** at least 190 of 200 (95%), tightened from 90%. The worst run lost 2; the bound
  allows 10.

A listener that waited on its flow's full queue (a mutation, not kept) failed the starvation test.

## T-004 -- 2026-10-23

The starvation test failed on CI's first run of M3 (`main` at 6d6d92c): 21, 21 and 37 of 200
exchanges answered across nextest's three tries, against 197 to 200 locally. The exchanging sender
shared the blaster's forward listener and echo server, and sampling `/proc/net/udp` during a local
run that lost 3 found the drops there: 170 384 on the shared listener's socket and 2 220 on the
shared echo socket, none on the exchanging sender's own. The blaster outruns the forward whenever
its receive loop is descheduled, and the kernel then drops arrivals at the full socket first come,
before sshunnel's per-flow queues see them; CI's busy 4-vCPU runner deschedules it far more often.
That is the kernel's queue, not the session's, so the test now gives each flow a forward and an
echo server of its own: what they still share is sshunnel's (the mux, the ssh pipe, the relay).
A mutation to one datagram queue shared by every flow fails it the way CI did (21 of 200), so it
still measures the session's fairness. The README says senders on one local port share its buffer.

## T-010 -- 2026-10-23

The root tests' first CI run (`main` at ce0b075) failed three of eight.

- **The two ping tests:** both the test's app and the relay (the remote is the runner itself) open
  unprivileged ping sockets as root, and the runner's `net.ipv4.ping_group_range` was `1 0`
  (nobody). The workflow now opens it before the root tests, as it opens user namespaces for the
  namespace tests: the remote allowing unprivileged ping is `route` ping's stated requirement.
- **`e2e_resolved_routes_only_the_patterns`:** the v6 fake range's reverse zone looked missing
  from the link, though the helper had set it (the root tests now run `route` with the helper's
  debug log, and no "fake IPv6 is off" line came). resolvectl wraps a link's domain list at the
  terminal's width, 80 without one (`status_print_strv_ifindex` in `resolvectl.c`), and the
  harness's `device_links` kept only the line naming the link, dropping the wrapped zone. It now
  folds the indented lines into their link's entry.
